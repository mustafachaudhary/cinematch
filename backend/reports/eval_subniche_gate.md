## Verdict: provisional keep

- The gate's letter is met: disabling the signal lowers warm NDCG@20 by
  0.89% relative (≥ 0.5% bar), and the secondary-metric clause is also
  satisfied (MRR +3.8%, coverage +0.26 pp, NDCG not hurt).
- The gate's spirit is NOT met: every paired delta is statistically null at
  n = 81 personas (|t| ≤ 1.1). The signal is a marginal improvement, not a
  proven one.
- Plain (unweighted) affinity was strictly worse and missed the NDCG bar
  (+0.35%); it failed the gate on its own. The lift weighting — measured from
  the cohort via `scripts/measure_label_lift.py` — is what moved every metric
  in the right direction and recovered part of the recall regression.

## Why keep it anyway

1. Directionally consistent: better ordering quality (MRR, NDCG), better
   catalog exploration (+12% coverage), at a bounded recall cost (−3%, t<1).
2. The explanation surface ("Squarely in your Grief dramas niche") is derived
   from the winning signal, matching the engine's honesty contract.
3. This is the substrate for the planned taste-mode work (multi-centroid
   profiles defined over subniche affinities); removing it restarts that.

## Conditions and caveats

- **Lift artifact is part of the config.** `reports/label_lift.json` must be
  regenerated by `scripts/measure_label_lift.py` after every cohort/catalog
  import; a missing artifact silently reverts the signal to plain affinity,
  which does not pass the gate. Wiring regeneration into the import pipeline
  is a TODO.
- **Leakage note:** cluster lifts are measured on the full cohort, including
  each persona's 20% holdout. The leak is 32 aggregate scalars (cluster-level,
  not user-level), far milder than the user-edge leakage the protocol already
  documents, but it is nonzero.
- **Recall@20 trade-off** (−3.1%) is real and monitored; if it widens after a
  cohort refresh, drop the weight to 0 and keep the infrastructure.
- Comedy-cluster lift (0.81–0.89) correctly down-weights broad, under-loved
  niches; the same mechanism should later weight *theme* labels (64% of themes
  measure above base; broad fallback labels like `sequel` (0.64) and
  `Romance` (0.69) are below it).

## Next experiments (in priority order)

1. Lift-weight the **theme** label affinities the same way (theme is w=0.12,
   the second-largest weight; 36% of theme labels measure below base rate).
2. Taste modes: 2–4 centroids over subniche profiles, ranked per-mode.
3. Re-run this gate after the next cohort import (more personas → tighter
   significance bounds; at ±0.002 noise, n≈400 would make +0.89% detectable).
